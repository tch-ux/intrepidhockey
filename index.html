The 1985/86 Hockey Almanac—still with me.

Story

40 Years of
The Details.

My obsession with NA hockey began in 1985, watching the World Championships in Prague from our TV at home as teenagers Mario Lemieux, Steve Yzerman and youngsters Ron Francis and Scott Stevens beat the Soviet Big Five. The following year, my dad bought me my first Hockey Almanac, the only available source about the NHL. I memorized every page.

In 1991, I watched the first-ever Czechoslovak TV coverage of the Stanley Cup Final as Mario and the Penguins won the Cup. But it was the 1993 World Championships that really stuck with me: I saw Paul Kariya—only an 18-year-old college freshman—dominate on the ice. It was my first real look at how the academic and hockey paths could merge at the highest level.

By the mid-90s, I was living it, attending school in the United States and finally seeing THE hockey in person. After my studies, I spent years as a professional in international education, consulting families on admissions to U.S. and Canadian schools. Today, some call me a "walking hockey encyclopedia," but for me, it's just the result of decades of studying the game.

At Intrepid Hockey, I combine that knowledge with my background in education to help families navigate the complex development pathways of hockey in North America. I still remain a diehard Mario and Kariya fan.
Go Black Bears!

Tomas Chadim
